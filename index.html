Natural Harmonics

A natural harmonic is the bell-like, flute-y tone you get by touching a string lightly at an exact point along its length — without pressing it to the fingerboard — instead of stopping it normally. An open string vibrates at its fundamental pitch, but it's also quietly vibrating in halves, thirds, quarters, and fifths at the same time (the harmonic series). Touching the string at one of those exact division points damps out the fundamental and lets that overtone ring alone, producing a pure, ringing pitch that's higher than — and different from — whatever note is normally at that fret.

Open string

Tuning trick: because the bass is tuned in perfect fourths, the harmonic at position 5 on a string always matches the harmonic at position 7 on the next higher string — e.g. the E string's position-5 harmonic and the A string's position-7 harmonic are the same pitch (E). Playing the two together and listening for beats is a quick way to check the bass is in tune. There's also a mirror-image node around position 19 (past this diagram) that reproduces the same pitch as position 7 — useful once you're working in upper/thumb position.
