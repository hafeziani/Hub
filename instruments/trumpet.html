Overtone (Harmonic) Series

A brass instrument doesn't need a new fingering for every note — for any single valve combination, changing lip tension and air speed alone moves you between different notes in that fingering's harmonic series (its overtones), the same way a bugle plays several notes with no valves at all. Pressing a valve just shifts the whole harmonic series down by a fixed amount, so the same lip-and-air pattern works from any fingering. Partials 7, 9, 11, 13, and 14 are naturally out of tune and skipped below — they're rarely used as reference pitches.

Overtone reachable on an open (no-valve) fingering by lip & air alone

Lip-slur trick: the open-valve lip slur from C4 up to G4 (partials 4→6) is the exact same muscle movement as the 1st-valve lip slur from B♭3 up to F4, or the 1-2-3 lip slur from C♯4 up to G♯4 — every fingering rides the same harmonic-series shape, just transposed. That's why lip slurs are practiced on one fingering at a time: it's the trumpet equivalent of the bass's cross-string harmonic pattern, done with breath instead of touch.
